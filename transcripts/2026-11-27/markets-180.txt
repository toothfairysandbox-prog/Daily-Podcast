Wall Street is rallying hard this morning, looking to close out a volatile week on a high note. The move is being driven by what traders are calling a Nvidia lifeline — some relief on the AI chipmaker after a brutal stretch that saw the stock tumble twenty-seven percent in a month. That said, Nvidia also just halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, so the AI story is still messy underneath the bounce. Jensen Huang faces questions from Congress this week, and how he handles that could set the tone for AI stocks into December. Separately, the S&P 500 has technically been in correction territory from the November selloff — worth remembering even as today looks green, since history says corrections don't automatically become bear markets.

Fifteen minutes into the session, the S&P 500 is up seven tenths of a percent, the Nasdaq is up about one percent, the Dow is up three quarters of a percent, and the Russell 2000 is leading large caps, up just over one percent. Overseas, the rally is far more dramatic — the Shanghai Composite is up three and three quarters percent, South Korea's Kospi is up over four percent, and Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng are each up around two percent. That's a serious risk-on move across Asia.

Energy and communication services are leading US sectors, both up about one and four tenths percent, with consumer discretionary close behind — all consistent with money flowing back into risk after the recent AI-driven scare. Technology, notably, is only up eight tenths of a percent, a more modest recovery than you'd expect given it's the sector that fell hardest. Health care and consumer staples are barely moving, a sign this is a return to risk rather than a broad rotation.

Treasuries are rallying too. The ten-year yield is down six basis points to four eighty-five, the thirty-year is also down six basis points to five twenty-four, and the two-year is down two basis points to four sixty-one. That's a bull steepener forming, consistent with easing growth fears alongside the equity bounce.

Watch Jensen Huang's congressional appearance this week — that's the real test of whether this rally has legs. Spain holds a snap election Sunday that could end Sánchez's government.
