Researchers engineered a molecular glue that restores normal chromosome connections in aging mouse eggs — addressing one of the real drivers of age-related fertility decline and miscarriage risk. It worked in mice; this is proof-of-concept, not a human fertility treatment yet, but it points toward something that could eventually improve IVF outcomes.

A few more worth knowing. Scientists got a tobacco-relative plant to synthesize paclitaxel, a major chemotherapy drug usually extracted expensively from yew trees — early-stage, but promising for cost and sustainability. And a study recording activity across forty-three mouse brain regions found most neurons are flexible generalists, not narrow specialists, challenging a long-standing assumption about brain organization.

One to hold loosely: new research links screen time during infancy and the start of formal schooling to weaker academic performance and working memory years later. It's an association from observational data, not proof screens caused it — worth tracking, not panicking over.

What to watch: alpha-gal, the tick-borne red meat allergy, keeps getting more diagnoses with the underlying biology still not well understood. And there's a short public quiz out now on dementia risk factors, if you want something concrete to act on this weekend.
