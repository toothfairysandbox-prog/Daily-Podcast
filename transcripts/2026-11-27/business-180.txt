Jensen Huang disappeared from view for eleven days this month, and it turned into an unplanned stress test for Nvidia. The board failed it. There's no clear successor, no bench anyone trusts to run a company this size, which means the market's trillion-dollar AI trade still rests on one man's health and mood. Huang's back now, and the usual showmanship is back with him, but the episode showed how fragile that arrangement is. Asian markets, heavily levered to the Nvidia trade, spent the stretch on a genuine rollercoaster.

That fragility showed up again this morning as stock futures surged, AI shares rebounding after weeks of turbulence that left the Nasdaq 100 on track for its worst month in nearly four years. It's a shortened trading day, so don't read too much into the bounce — but it tells you how jumpy this sector still is.

Elsewhere: Altria is exploring a takeover of Imperial Brands, a roughly 30-billion-dollar target, which would be a serious bet that tobacco consolidation still has room to run even as volumes shrink globally. Rheinmetall cut its midterm sales and margin forecasts after Germany canceled a frigate order and delayed a combat-vehicle program — a reminder that Europe's defense boom is real but lumpy, dependent on governments that can still change their minds. And Norse Atlantic, the budget transatlantic carrier, halted operations over Thanksgiving, warning that rising fuel costs could push it into bankruptcy without a rescue.

The thread connecting a lot of this is sudden exposure to single points of failure — one executive, one government contract, one fuel-price swing — in businesses that looked sturdier a month ago. Allstate's customers have spent days unable to reach the insurer during a multiday tech outage, right in the middle of the year's busiest travel week. If I'm wrong about this being a pattern, it'll be because these turn out to be unrelated bad weeks rather than a broader fragility story.

On the calendar: watch how AI stocks hold up once full trading resumes next week, and whether Altria moves from exploring to formally bidding on Imperial Brands. Holiday spending data through the long Black Friday weekend will also start rolling in, testing whether inflation-driven bargain-hunting translates into real retail strength.
