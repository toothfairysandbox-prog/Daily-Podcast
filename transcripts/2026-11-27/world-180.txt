Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky used a BBC interview to vent real frustration, calling the relationship between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin "strange" and saying Washington isn't pushing Moscow hard enough to end the war. That frustration landed the same day European Union leaders wrapped a summit in Brussels without a deal — twelve-plus hours of talks produced no agreement on the bloc's next budget or on a forty-nine billion dollar loan meant to cover Ukraine's military and economic needs next year. Meanwhile Russia keeps hitting Ukraine's energy grid, and residents are bracing for another winter of strikes and blackouts, with food prices rising and people stocking up on essentials. The money and the military pressure both look stuck at the same moment Kyiv needs both.

In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot's push to unseat Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu picked up real momentum — his Yashar party struck a preliminary coalition deal with a left-wing faction after a brief internal dispute. But some of his own allies are now floating power-sharing with Netanyahu instead, which would break a core campaign promise. North Korea's Kim Jong Un responded to Trump's recent overture by doing the opposite of de-escalating — he visited facilities that make nuclear materials and called for building more weapons, citing threats from the US and South Korea. And in eastern Congo, the Ebola outbreak has crossed twelve thousand cases, with some aid responders now evacuating as fighting between rebels and the army blocks containment work.

Slower-moving: the United Nations says famine risk is rising globally, driven by conflict and El Nino, with three of four newly identified hunger hotspots in Africa. It's not a single headline event, it's a slow accumulation, and the marker to watch is whether any of those hotspots tips into a formally declared famine.

Watch the Spain election Sunday, where housing and immigration have dominated a bitter campaign that could end Pedro Sanchez's government. And the UN Secretary-General's term ends this year with no agreed successor — a vacancy nobody has solved.
