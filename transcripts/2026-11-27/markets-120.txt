Wall Street is rallying to close out a volatile week, driven by what's being called a Nvidia lifeline after the stock's twenty-seven percent monthly tumble. But Nvidia also just halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, and Jensen Huang faces Congress this week — the real test of whether this bounce holds. The S&P 500 did slip into correction territory this month, a reminder the AI jitters are real even as today looks strong.

Fifteen minutes in, the S&P 500 is up seven tenths of a percent, the Nasdaq up about one percent, the Dow up three quarters, and the Russell 2000 leading, up just over one percent. Overseas the move is bigger: Shanghai is up nearly four percent, South Korea's Kospi over four percent, and Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng each up around two percent.

Energy and communication services lead US sectors, both up roughly one and four tenths percent, with consumer discretionary close behind — classic risk-on flow. Technology's only up eight tenths percent, lagging given it fell hardest in November. Health care and staples are flat, suggesting this is relief buying, not a broad rotation.

Treasuries are rallying — the ten-year down six basis points to four eighty-five, the thirty-year down six to five twenty-four, the two-year down two to four sixty-one. A bull steepener, consistent with easing growth worries alongside the equity bounce.
