Ukraine has confirmed what Russia wouldn't: its forces struck two Russian naval vessels off the coast of Libya on Sunday, sinking the corvette Merkury with seven domestically made sea drones launched from a mothership. President Volodymyr Zelensky claimed responsibility directly, and that's the real story here — Kyiv isn't hiding this one. The Merkury was one of Russia's newer warships, docked in Benghazi on its way home. The Kremlin has warned of "escalation" in the region but won't address the attack specifically. The message, as France 24 put it, is that Ukraine can now reach Russian assets almost anywhere. That fits a wider pattern NPR flagged: this war keeps bleeding beyond Ukraine's borders, with missile incidents over Poland and alleged Russian operations inside Denmark. Meanwhile the war at home grinds on — a Russian strike on Dymer killed eleven people, though rescuers managed to pull a seven-year-old boy alive from the rubble.

Elsewhere: the longest United Nations climate conference on record ended in Antalya with no deal, countries unable to bridge divides over fossil fuels. Australia's prime minister, co-host of COP31, blamed "difficult geopolitical times." Critics call it proof the whole process now protects fossil-fuel interests while leaving vulnerable nations to absorb the cost. In Belgium, a national strike against austerity measures has grounded flights and shut down rail service, with the government's survival now in question. And in Indonesia, the death toll from a confectionery factory fire has reached 24, with roughly 30 people still missing.

One to watch longer-term: Iran says it will use "all available means" to counter what it calls a US naval blockade, language that's escalated sharply in recent days without yet turning into direct confrontation — worth watching for whether that changes.

Keep an eye on Belgium's government, which could fall over this budget standoff, and on whether Russia responds militarily to the Libya strikes.
