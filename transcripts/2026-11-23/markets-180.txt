Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO this weekend, reportedly with input from senior levels of the U.S. government, and the stock is up nearly thirty percent in early trading, adding almost a trillion dollars in market value and clawing back almost everything it lost since his affair disclosure. That one stock is dragging the entire market with it. Separately, CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler in an all-stock deal worth about forty-four billion dollars, betting that AI-driven cyberattacks mean cybersecurity defense only gets more valuable from here. And health insurers are sliding after word that the U.S. is targeting Medicare Advantage ratings, with UnitedHealth, Humana and CVS all under pressure on practice scrutiny.

Sixteen minutes into the session, the Nasdaq is up five point nine percent, the S&P five hundred up four point two percent, the Dow up two point four percent, and the Russell two thousand up three point two percent. Overseas, it's a split picture: European markets are solidly higher, but Japan's Nikkei closed down just over five percent and South Korea's KOSPI fell three point three percent, both likely catching the tail end of last week's AI-stock rout before the Huang news turned U.S. sentiment around.

Technology is the standout sector, up nearly eight percent on Nvidia alone pulling chip and AI names higher. Consumer discretionary and industrials are both up around three percent, riding the broader risk-on mood. On the other end, consumer staples is actually down slightly, money rotating out of defensive names and into the rally. Energy is barely moving, with crude oil down two point seven percent as supply gains outweigh Middle East disruption worries.

Treasuries are rallying too: the two-year yield down thirteen basis points to four point six three percent, the ten-year down twelve basis points to four point nine two percent, the thirty-year down nine basis points to five point three one percent. The curve is steepening a touch, with yields falling as risk appetite returns rather than fear driving a flight to safety.

Worth noting: Friday's VIX spike, now unwinding fast, down over twenty-five percent today, has historically marked a buying opportunity rather than the start of something worse. Watch this Thanksgiving week for Dell and HPE earnings, a real test of whether AI infrastructure demand matches the hype reigniting today.
