Nvidia's board has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, a little more than a week after he resigned amid allegations of multiple affairs, including with a direct report. The board said it voted unanimously to bring him back after a series of discussions left it, in its words, satisfied with his candor and his ability to lead the company. Reporting also points to senior officials in the US government backing the reversal. Whatever the internal story, the practical effect is that the world's most valuable company just spent a week and a half without settled leadership during a period when every major AI lab and cloud provider is racing to lock down chip supply. That instability, even brief, is the real story here: Nvidia's roadmap, its relationships with TSMC and its customers, and its credibility with Washington all run through Huang personally, and the board clearly decided it couldn't afford to find out what the company looks like without him.

A few other things worth knowing. Sierra, the enterprise customer experience company co-founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, just raised one point seven five billion dollars in a Series F, doubling its valuation to thirty billion dollars in six months. That's a real signal that AI deployed for customer service, not just chatbots, is finding paying enterprise customers fast. Separately, three of Anthropic's early backers, Notable Capital, GIC, and Baillie Gifford, are now talking publicly about what convinced them to bet billions ahead of its IPO, which tells you that offering is closer than it might seem. And a new coding model called GLM 5.4, built around what its maker calls a loop transformer architecture, is drawing serious attention on Hacker News for its approach to generalized coding, not just benchmark chasing.

OpenAI's chief scientist floated ideas this week for putting a speed limit on AI development. It's an interesting thought experiment, but the piece itself admits the hard part: nobody agrees on how to even measure the speed you'd be limiting. Until that's solved, this is a conversation, not a policy.

Worth watching this week: whether Nvidia's stock and its customer contracts show any lasting effect from the leadership scare, and whether more companies follow Sierra and Anthropic in using this moment to raise money while AI enterprise demand is hot.
