Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, days after its chairman said the board had no intention of bringing him back. The company cited input from senior levels of the US government, and Trump called it the correct decision. Huang returns as CEO, president and board director despite the affairs scandal that triggered his removal. The stock jumped thirty percent. The real story: Nvidia's value is so concentrated in one man that the company couldn't let the controversy stand, and outside pressure got it reversed.

Elsewhere, CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler in a forty-four billion dollar all-stock deal, combining two major cybersecurity names as AI threats climb the boardroom agenda. Texas Pacific Land is paying roughly three billion in stock for EagleRock Land, a water-and-land management firm. And in the UK, Capita's shares collapsed seventy percent after its CEO quit over a botched civil service pension scheme that delayed payments to public workers.

A pattern: companies leaning on single points of failure. Nvidia on Huang, Capita on one contract, even the Eric Trump-backed robot startup Foundation cutting employee health benefits while it waits on a Korean investor. That concentration is fragile — worth watching whether diversified firms start outperforming.
