A 244 patient Phase 2 trial from Kymera Therapeutics found its experimental pill effectively treated eczema, positioning it as a possible oral rival to Dupixent, which has to be injected. It's solid mid-stage data, not proof it beats an established injectable — larger trials are still ahead. But a pill alternative to a shot is the kind of edge that matters commercially.

A few others: Invivyd reported its monoclonal antibody strongly protected people against Covid, in company-reported results, useful for people who don't respond well to vaccines. And Scientific American's look at the Ebola outbreak notes that vaccine trials are underway, but experts say what's really needed is public health infrastructure, not just a medical fix. Separately, the federal panel that recommends preventive screenings met for the first time in over a year, and cast its votes in private amid concerns about how the process has been reshaped politically.

On the overhyped side: a phase three trial found an erectile dysfunction drug showed early promise against Alzheimer's. One early trial, and plenty of past Alzheimer's candidates have looked good early and then faded. Worth watching, not a breakthrough yet.

Watch for Kymera's drug advancing to larger trials next.
