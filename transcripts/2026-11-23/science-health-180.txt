A 244 patient Phase 2 trial from Kymera Therapeutics found its experimental pill cleared up itchy, inflamed skin in eczema patients about as well as safety allowed, positioning it as a possible oral rival to Dupixent, which currently has to be injected. This is mid-stage data — it shows the drug works and is reasonably safe in a few hundred people, not that it will beat an established injectable in the larger trials still to come. But a pill alternative to a twice-monthly shot is the kind of convenience edge that moves markets and prescriptions alike.

A few other items worth flagging. Invivyd says its monoclonal antibody strongly protected people against Covid in company-reported results — useful for immunocompromised people who don't respond well to vaccines, though it's one company's own readout and whether there's commercial demand for another antibody at this stage of the pandemic is a real question. Separately, Scientific American looked at what's actually driving the current Ebola outbreak, and the answer experts give isn't just virology — vaccine trials are underway, but containing it needs public health infrastructure and trust, not just a shot. And the first meeting in over a year of the federal panel that recommends preventive care — things like cancer screenings — happened with votes cast privately, amid concern about how RFK Junior's changes have reshaped the process.

On the overhyped side: a phase three trial found an erectile dysfunction drug showed early promise against Alzheimer's. Early results, one trial, and a long history of Alzheimer's candidates that looked good in early data and then didn't pan out. Interesting, not proof of anything yet.

Also quietly fun: scientists sequenced the genome of Jonathan the tortoise, 194 years old, and found variants tied to DNA repair, inflammation control, and cancer suppression — a real clue into how extreme longevity works at the genetic level, even if it won't translate into anything for humans anytime soon.

Watch for Kymera's drug moving into larger trials, and for more detail on how that preventive services panel's private votes play out.
