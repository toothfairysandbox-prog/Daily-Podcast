Anthropic's IPO is the story everyone's watching this morning. The company sold more shares than planned, at a higher price than expected, and is set to debut on Wall Street in the top ten most valuable US companies by market cap. Seven founders are about to become multi-billionaires on paper. The real test, though, is whether Anthropic can turn surging enterprise demand for Claude into profitability that justifies the price, because right now the bet is almost entirely on future growth. Worth noting: Wall Street firms pledged five hundred billion dollars toward Nvidia-related AI infrastructure, and so far, not a dollar of it has actually been deployed. The gap between AI pledges and AI spending is getting wide.

Away from AI, the consumer data told a clearer story. Walmart's revenue rose five point two percent to a hundred eighty-eight point eight billion dollars, powered by e-commerce, and it raised full-year guidance. Target's shares jumped seven percent on its best sales growth in five years, also with a guidance hike. Lowe's did the same despite a soft housing market. Three retailers, all beating and raising, all leaning on online sales. That's a tell: shoppers are still spending, but they want it delivered, not browsed.

Alibaba posted a sharp earnings rebound, crediting improved AI profitability, even as scrutiny over chip exports to China intensifies. And Waymo pulled its robotaxis off US freeways nationwide after California regulators called them an imminent hazard following construction-zone incidents — a real check on the robotaxi timeline.

The pattern: AI spending is still mostly promises — pledges, hires, IPO hype — while the actual profit engine right now is old-fashioned retail execution. I'd be wrong if that five hundred billion starts moving fast, or if Anthropic's first earnings as a public company show real margin, not just revenue growth.

Watch for Anthropic's stock debut and early trading reaction, plus more AI-linked earnings as the enterprise spending story gets tested in real numbers.
