Anthropic priced its IPO above expectations and sold more shares than planned, putting it on track to debut among the ten most valuable companies on Wall Street. Seven founders become multi-billionaires. But the real question is whether enterprise demand for Claude converts into sustainable profit — right now it's a bet on growth. Context worth holding onto: Wall Street pledged five hundred billion dollars toward Nvidia-linked AI buildout, and none of it has been deployed yet.

Meanwhile the consumer numbers landed cleaner. Walmart's revenue rose five point two percent to a hundred eighty-eight point eight billion dollars on e-commerce strength, and raised guidance. Target jumped seven percent on its best sales growth in five years. Lowe's also raised its outlook despite a weak housing market. Online sales are doing the heavy lifting at all three.

Alibaba's earnings rebounded sharply on improved AI profitability, even as chip-export scrutiny builds. And Waymo pulled robotaxis off US freeways nationwide after California regulators called them an imminent hazard.

The split: AI money is still mostly pledges and IPO hype, while retail is delivering actual, measured profit through e-commerce. Watch whether that five hundred billion starts moving, and how Anthropic trades once it's actually public.
