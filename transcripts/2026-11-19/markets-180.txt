Anthropic priced its IPO this morning, and it's set to be the biggest in history, bigger than SpaceX's. The AI trade, which has looked shaky the last couple weeks, just got a jolt of confidence from it — this is a company that could mint thousands of multimillionaire employees, and the market is reading it as a vote that the AI buildout still has legs. Separately, the US named Salesforce, OpenAI and Nvidia to a new AI export consortium, and Intel said it's landed its first outside customer for its advanced eighteen-A chips. Barclays, meanwhile, is out with a warning worth noting: if the AI trade actually does unwind, the other things holding up this economy don't have much underneath them either. They're not calling for a collapse — they see the recent wobble as a buyable correction — but it's a flag on how narrow this rally really is.

Nineteen minutes into the session, the S&P is up about one percent, the Nasdaq is leading with a gain near one point seven percent on that AI reignition, the Dow is barely positive, up about a tenth of a percent, and the Russell is up roughly half a percent. Overseas, Hong Kong's Hang Seng is the standout mover, down close to two percent, while European markets are flat to slightly lower and Japan's Nikkei is up over seven tenths of a percent.

Technology is running away with it, up nearly three percent on the Anthropic pop. Communication services and energy are following at a more modest clip. On the other end, consumer staples are down close to two percent and health care is off over one percent — classic money rotating out of defensives and into growth on a risk-on morning.

Treasuries are rallying. The ten-year is down three basis points to five point zero seven percent, the thirty-year down five to five point four one, while the two-year sits unmoved at four point eight percent. That's the curve steepening a touch, consistent with the Fed minutes from Wednesday showing officials still weighing how to handle future shocks.
