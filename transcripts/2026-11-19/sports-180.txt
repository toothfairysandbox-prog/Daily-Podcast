Luka Doncic finally hit a wall. After a stretch of forty and fifty point nights, the Lakers ran into the Clippers and Doncic looked human for once — legs gone, shots short — and the Lakers lost. It's one game in an otherwise ridiculous stretch, but it's the first real evidence there's a limit to what he can carry alone.

Around the rest of the NBA: the Wizards snapped an eight-game losing streak beating a Raptors team missing their three best players, with Dybantsa and Davis doing the scoring. The Sixers erased a seventeen-point deficit for a shorthanded win over Orlando. The Suns found their offensive rhythm by passing up good shots for great ones. The Rockets beat the Pacers a hundred and nineteen to a hundred and four, but it cost them — Alperen Sengun went down injured. The Nets, at three and eleven, still look lost with no identity on either end. And Jayson Tatum is disputing the story of his technical foul against Denver, insisting he never threw the ball at the referee, just that it looked bad.

The longer story in Boston and beyond: Denver's win over the Celtics came with real officiating grumbling, Portland's season is quietly interesting again with Damian Lillard back from his Achilles tear and already shifting the Trail Blazers' identity even short of his old peak — worth tracking as he builds toward full speed.

Thursday night football has Indianapolis at Houston, and in the NFL more broadly, Dallas is under real pressure — Quinnen Williams spent his press conference refusing to discuss a defensive play-calling change, repeating the same evasive line a dozen times, which tells you something's unsettled there. Nick Bosa admitted he's struggling to get his pass rush working since returning from injury. On Saturday, England make nine changes to face the All Blacks with Fin Smith restored and Henry Pollock dropped to the bench, and Ireland's Dan Sheehan starts out of position at number eight against world champion South Africa in Dublin. Tonight, Edmonton visits Tampa Bay in a legitimate Stanley Cup Finals preview — that's the one worth your evening.
