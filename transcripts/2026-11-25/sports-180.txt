Aaron Rodgers didn't show up to the Steelers' Wednesday morning meeting, a day after Mike McCarthy benched him, and he wasn't at the team facility either. The benching itself came out of nowhere Wednesday morning, and by all accounts it's landed badly in that locker room. Rodgers is a four-time MVP, and the read growing across the league this morning is that this might actually be the last game he ever plays in the NFL. McCarthy is expected to face the media at some point today, and whatever he says will matter less than whether Rodgers ever says anything back.

Elsewhere, the NFL's Thanksgiving slate is shaping up as the real story of the week. Bills and Chiefs renew their rivalry with Buffalo sitting at nine and one atop the AFC and Kansas City a game behind, and it's being billed as the biggest regular-season game of the year. Packers and Rams meet in a game that matters plenty for the NFC wild card, with the Rams banged up at receiver. Bears at Lions and Boston College at Miami round out the holiday football on tap. Off the field, Raheem Sterling, the former England international, was given a suspended twelve-month jail sentence for dangerous driving and possession of nitrous oxide after a motorway crash in a Lamborghini.

The Rodgers saga is really the story behind every other NFL story this week. He goes from four MVPs to benched without warning, reportedly blindsided his own front office with a parting message before it happened, and now the question is whether a team claims him or whether this is just how a Hall of Fame career ends, quietly, in a morning meeting he didn't attend.

Thanksgiving football is the thing to watch tonight and tomorrow. Bills-Chiefs on Thanksgiving weekend carries real AFC playoff stakes, and Packers-Rams tonight could shake up the NFC wild card picture before the turkey's even out of the oven.
