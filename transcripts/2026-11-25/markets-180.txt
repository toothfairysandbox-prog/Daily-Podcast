The Fed's preferred inflation gauge came in hot this morning: the PCE price index accelerated to 3.8% annually in October, right at consensus but moving the wrong direction, and it raises real questions about how much room the Fed has to cut next year. That hit stocks hard. It's landing alongside the fallout from Nvidia's Jensen Huang saga — Huang resigned amid reports of affairs including one with a Chinese businesswoman alleged to have ties to Beijing intelligence, then was reinstated after what CNBC describes as direct pressure from Trump, and that whiplash is now drawing bipartisan scrutiny over how close Washington and big tech have become. Separately, Washington proposed fresh tariffs on fourteen trading partners including China and the European Union, reviving a trade fight the Supreme Court had partly struck down earlier this year. On the brighter side, third-quarter GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annual pace, the fastest in three years.

None of that brighter data is helping this morning. Sixteen minutes into the session, the S&P 500 is down three percent, the Nasdaq is getting hit hardest at down four and a half percent, the Dow is off two percent, and the Russell 2000 is down one and a half. Overseas, Japan's Nikkei fell nearly three percent, but the real story is South Korea — the KOSPI is down over six percent on the day.

It's a tech-led rout. The tech sector is down over five percent, with AI-infrastructure names like CoreWeave and Nebius sliding and Dell falling despite reporting sales more than doubling, on worries about power and capital access behind its backlog. Consumer staples, real estate and utilities are the only sectors holding up, which is classic defensive positioning when growth names get hit. Energy's only down slightly, helped by crude up nearly a percent.

Treasuries are fairly calm given the equity chaos: the ten-year actually eased one basis point to 4.91%, the two-year is flat at 4.63%, and the thirty-year ticked up a basis point to 5.30%. That's not a growth-scare curve — it's a market digesting sticky inflation more than panicking about recession. The VIX, though, is up over twenty percent, so expect a choppy day. Bitcoin is down nearly four percent too, testing the $58,000 level, with real ETF outflows behind it.
