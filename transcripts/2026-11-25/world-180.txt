President Trump's administration has proposed a fresh round of tariffs on some of its largest trading partners, including the European Union, Japan and Taiwan, with China facing a possible twenty percent rate. It lands just weeks after a cost-of-living backlash cost Republicans a sweeping defeat in the midterms, and it signals the administration isn't backing off confrontation on trade even with that political wound fresh. Expect retaliation threats from Brussels and Beijing in the coming days.

In Ukraine, leaked Russian data obtained and reported on shows Moscow has lost nearly five hundred thousand people in the war, a figure that reveals how deeply the invasion has reshaped Russian mortality patterns, regardless of what the Kremlin says publicly. Separately, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is pushing hard for fast-tracked NATO membership and more air defense, but a NATO Parliamentary Assembly meeting in Berlin made clear the unanimity needed just isn't there yet. And North Korea appears to be holding back on sending Russia the additional troops Kyiv warned about months ago, with analysts saying Pyongyang is carefully balancing its relationships with both Moscow and Beijing.

In Colombia, security forces freed all roughly twenty hostages held for seventeen hours at a supermarket in Cartagena; one gunman was killed in the raid. President Abelardo de la Espriella said the captors were common criminals fleeing a botched armored truck robbery, not part of any broader plot.

Watch China's internal politics: Beijing is investigating Politburo member and Shanghai party chief Chen Jining, the latest senior official caught in Xi Jinping's anti-corruption campaign, which keeps widening even as it approaches the top ranks of the party.

Ahead this week: watch for how the EU and China respond to Trump's tariff announcement, and whether Huawei's conviction for trade secret theft in a New York court — alongside its acquittal on racketeering — adds fresh friction to the broader US-China tech standoff.
