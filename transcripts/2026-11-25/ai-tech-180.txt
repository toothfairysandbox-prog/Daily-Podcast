Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia's board just three days ago is already looking shakier. At an all-hands meeting Tuesday, Huang thanked the White House for backing his reinstatement, apologized to staff, and said he's in therapy. That's an extraordinary thing for a sitting CEO to say out loud, and it tells you how serious the underlying mess is: Huang's personal relationship with Alice Huang, an executive with alleged ties to Chinese intelligence, connected to a company accused of illegally diverting Nvidia chips to China. Trump has reportedly floated pardoning Huang as "an option," which only underscores that this isn't settled. Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld is now arguing the board that reinstated Huang faces real legal exposure, calling Nvidia a "detriment" to American industrial strength rather than a champion of it. Huang, for his part, is still resisting calls for a succession plan, saying it creates unnecessary hierarchy — a strange position for a company that just spent two weeks finding out what happens without him.

Elsewhere, Amazon and Meta are reportedly negotiating to end a two-month standoff over Meta's Muse AI shopping agent, which Amazon has been blocking from its marketplace. The sticking points are reportedly which products Muse recommends and what that does to Amazon's ad business — a preview of how every retailer is going to have to negotiate terms with AI agents shopping on a customer's behalf. Microsoft, meanwhile, declared an internal "Code Red" after a bug sitting undetected since July triggered a nearly day-long Outlook and Exchange outage affecting millions.

Separately, Amazon disclosed that hackers used AI agents to find and exploit a zero-day vulnerability with very little human guidance — a sign the same automation helping defenders is now helping attackers move faster, too.

The amount of attention on Huang's personal life risks obscuring the actual governance question: whether Nvidia's board exercised independent judgment or just folded to political pressure. That's the story worth watching, not the therapy line.

Watch for how the Amazon-Meta talks resolve — the terms could set the template for every retailer's relationship with shopping agents going forward.
