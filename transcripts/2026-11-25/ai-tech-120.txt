Jensen Huang's reinstatement at Nvidia is getting messier by the day. At an all-hands Tuesday, he thanked the White House for backing his return and said he's in therapy — remarkable for a sitting CEO, and a sign of how serious the underlying issue is: his relationship with Alice Huang, an executive with alleged ties to Chinese intelligence, tied to a company accused of diverting Nvidia chips to China. Trump has reportedly floated pardoning Huang as "an option." Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld argues the board now faces real legal exposure for backing him, calling Nvidia a "detriment," not a champion, of American industrial strength. Huang is still resisting a succession plan, which is a strange stance after two weeks of proving the company can't easily run without him.

Separately, Amazon and Meta are reportedly negotiating to end a two-month block on Meta's Muse shopping agent, with Amazon's ad revenue and product recommendations as the sticking points — a preview of how every retailer will have to deal with AI agents shopping on customers' behalf. Microsoft also declared an internal "Code Red" after a bug dormant since July caused a nearly day-long Outlook outage.

The Huang personal-life coverage is crowding out the real question: did Nvidia's board actually exercise judgment here, or just bow to political pressure? That's what's worth watching as this plays out.
