The Army detonated a cache of degraded lead azide in Iowa this morning, and health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Junior has weighed in as experts warn of real health risks from the toxic fallout. The Iowa Poison Control Center has guidance out today for nearby residents — this is live and worth tracking through the day.

On firmer ground: researchers have found a new class of potent antibodies that neutralize diverse HIV strains, a genuine shift toward believing an HIV vaccine is achievable. Real hurdles remain between this and an actual shot.

A study of over ten thousand people found gut bacteria pick up specific mutations as we age, though few were linked to disease — more a window into aging than a health warning.

In England, heat-linked deaths hit a record five thousand two hundred this year, double the prior record, per the UK Health Security Agency.

And a new nasal treatment restored youthful memory in aging mice and slowed Alzheimer's-like decline using mRNA-style tech. Striking in mice, but mouse brains aren't human ones — plenty of nose-drop neuroscience has failed that jump before.

Meanwhile, doctors are fighting disinformation around vitamin K shots for newborns, which prevent a rare but serious bleeding disorder, as refusals land some babies in intensive care.
