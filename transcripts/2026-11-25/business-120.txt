The US announced new tariffs overnight on a long list of partners, including China and the EU — a signal the tariff era is expanding, not winding down. Expect retaliation threats and repricing to follow.

In Britain, Ofgem is raising the energy price cap, with typical household bills rising twenty-six percent from January, from seventeen twenty-three to over twenty-one hundred pounds a year, driven by a gas price surge.

Nvidia's troubles keep compounding. Huawei was convicted of fraud in a US criminal trial, part of a wider tangle of chip-related legal risk. Jensen Huang, reinstated as CEO after Trump intervened, is trying to project business as usual while lawmakers demand answers and the Justice Department publicly defends the company.

On the brighter side, Best Buy posted comparable sales growth of six-point-one percent, its best in five years, and raised guidance — part of a broader pattern of real retail turnarounds this holiday season, not just easy comparisons. Stripe is in talks that would value it above two hundred billion dollars.

The thread: consumers are spending, but getting squeezed from multiple directions — tariffs looming, energy costs rising, secondhand shopping going mainstream as people chase value. Watch whether retail strength holds once those costs actually land.
