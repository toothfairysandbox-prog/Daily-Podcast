Donald Trump is in Asia for the diplomatic event of his term so far: a trilateral with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit in Manila, after stops tied to the APEC meeting in Asia this week. It's his first trip abroad since Republicans took a beating in the midterms, and several analysts reading the trip see a president pivoting toward foreign policy now that his domestic hand is weaker. Nothing is scheduled to be signed, but a sit-down with both Xi and Putin at once, so soon after a bruising election, is itself the story — it signals where Trump thinks he can still make headlines even if he can't move Congress.

Ukraine delivered its biggest message of the war's drone campaign to date: Russia says Kyiv launched over two thousand drones in a single day, including a strike that hit the Crimea bridge, with Russia logging a record seventeen air raid alerts. Ukrainian officials are framing it plainly — if Russia keeps hitting Ukrainian bridges and infrastructure, Ukraine can reach Russian ones too. It's a signal of capability as much as a tactical strike, and it raises the question of how Moscow responds.

In Yemen, the BBC's security correspondent warns that Iran-backed Houthi forces are gaining ground again, a shift that could ripple into shipping lanes and global trade given their history of targeting Red Sea traffic. And five EU countries — Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Denmark and Greece — have announced a deal to send rejected asylum seekers to a new "return hub" in Rwanda. Critics are already calling it a grave moral failing, and legal challenges are expected; it's the most aggressive outsourcing of EU migration policy yet attempted.

Sudan's civil war keeps grinding on largely out of view. Rights group Emergency Lawyers says an army air attack killed thirteen people, including women and children, in West Kordofan — one more entry in a conflict that's displaced millions without a ceasefire in sight. The real marker to watch there isn't any single strike, it's whether outside powers ever force the two sides back to talks.

This week's calendar is packed: watch for whatever actually comes out of the Trump-Xi-Putin conversations in Manila, and for how Moscow answers Ukraine's drone campaign. Also in Geneva, countries are negotiating rules on autonomous weapons — so-called killer robots — with the US, Russia and Israel resisting anything legally binding.
