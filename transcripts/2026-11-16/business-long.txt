Nvidia's board has told allies of Jensen Huang it is not considering bringing him back as chief executive. That's a direct answer to a campaign that's gotten surprisingly loud: billionaire backers, tech figures, even Trump, publicly pushing for his reinstatement. The pressure comes right as new CEO Colette Kress heads into her first earnings call without Huang, with legal scrutiny still hanging over his relationship with a woman suspected of working with Chinese intelligence. Wall Street will be watching whether Kress can hold investor confidence that Fortune describes as having undergone, in one analyst's words, a complete evaporation. The board's answer suggests Nvidia thinks the company is bigger than one founder, even one this identified with it. That's a real test of whether that's true.

Elsewhere, Quebecor made a twenty-one-point-nine-six-billion-dollar bid for Telus, a deal that would stitch together a pan-Canadian telecom giant out of Telus's western footprint and Quebecor's strength in the east. It's the kind of consolidation play that tends to draw regulatory attention fast.

Technology also had an ugly Monday on the operations side. United Airlines resolved a system outage that delayed departures by knocking out tools pilots use to communicate with dispatch, just days after a federal ground stop disrupted schedules nationwide. And Chase, the country's largest bank, along with JPMorgan and Wells Fargo customers, reported being locked out of their own money, blamed on a software bug the bank says wasn't systemic or cyber-related. Two separate glitches, same message: the plumbing behind daily commerce is fragile in ways customers only notice when it breaks.

That's the pattern worth flagging: it's been a rough stretch for infrastructure reliability across sectors that have no business failing quietly. Airlines, banks, and now gold and silver hitting thirteen-month lows on rate-hike expectations — all signs of a system under more strain than it looks on a calm day. I'd rethink that if the outages turn out to be unrelated one-offs rather than a stress pattern.

On the calendar: Nvidia's earnings call looms as the week's biggest test, and watch for regulatory movement on that Quebecor-Telus bid.
