A federal approval leads today. The FDA cleared a new breast cancer drug from Roche, giving some patients another treatment option and giving Roche a medicine it's betting could become its best-selling product ever. That's a pharma headline as much as a medical one — watch for what the actual trial data show about survival benefit as the drug reaches the clinic, not just the sales projections.

A few other things worth flagging. Scientists have found Neanderthal DNA inherited by modern humans is associated with lower schizophrenia risk — an association from genetic data, not proof that any single gene variant protects anyone, but it adds to a growing picture of how ancient interbreeding still shapes our biology. Separately, researchers are reporting a biological divide in Alzheimer's and chronic pain tied to sex, genetics and menopause — early work, but it's pointing toward why conditions like fibromyalgia hit women harder and why treatments might need to be tailored rather than one-size-fits-all. And in supplement news, the FDA has created a new regulatory category for NAC, a compound that's been sold for years in a legal gray area — a regulatory fix, not a new finding about whether it works for anything.

Here's the one to be skeptical about: hormone therapy after menopause being linked to better cognition. The coverage makes it sound like a straightforward brain boost, but the researchers themselves say the effect depends heavily on genetics and on which type of hormone therapy is used — this is not a case where more data points to "everyone should take it." If you're already on hormone therapy, this is a research finding, not a reason to change anything, and it's worth remembering observational hormone studies have a long history of looking better in early analysis than they hold up later.

One more genuinely interesting result: a virus that infected chimpanzee ancestors millions of years ago appears to have switched off a gene that stayed active in humans, a gene that may be involved in early brain development. It's a single-lab finding at this stage, but it's a real contribution to the question of what actually makes human brains different from our closest relatives.

What to watch: the Leonid meteor shower peaked overnight, worth a look if skies are clear, and that Roche drug's uptake and pricing will be the thing to track as it reaches oncologists in the coming months.
