Russia hit Ukraine with one of its biggest air attacks yet overnight, killing at least six people in Kyiv, and two Russian missiles landed in southeastern Poland. Warsaw's prime minister, Donald Tusk, called it a provocation against NATO territory, though no one was hurt and no damage was reported. The strikes came hours after Vladimir Putin vowed a "painful" response to Ukraine's attack on the Crimea bridge. European leaders, meeting in Berlin, say they now have a united position for talks with Russia on ending the war, but Putin has separately proposed evacuating the Ukrainian city of Kostiantynivka, a plan Kyiv and Western analysts suspect is cover for a military operation rather than a humanitarian one, echoing what happened in Oleshky.

Britain and the European Union struck what both sides are calling a landmark deal, a decade after the Brexit vote. It scraps checks on plant and animal trade, links their carbon markets, and opens a youth mobility scheme letting a hundred thousand young people move between the two. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is framing it as the start of undoing years of damage, though plenty in Britain will see it as drifting back toward the bloc by stealth.

The International Court of Justice ruled Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, ordering restitution and reparations. Myanmar's government and China have rejected the ruling as unjust; Rohingya advocates welcomed it but warn enforcement will be slow and difficult.

At COP31 in Turkey, talks ran into overtime with the draft deal still making no mention of fossil fuels, and African nations pressing, again, for climate finance that has yet to materialize.

Watch for how Europe's push for Russia talks collides with Moscow's evacuation plan for Kostiantynivka — whether it becomes a genuine negotiating track or another contested city falls under the guise of rescue.
