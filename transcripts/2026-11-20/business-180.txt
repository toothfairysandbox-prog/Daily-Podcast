Anthropic's shares fell today, just days after a blockbuster opening, and it's dragging other tech names down with it. The read here matters: Anthropic's IPO was supposed to be the moment AI moved from hype to hard numbers, what one analysis called the "show me the money, for real" era. Investors showing up and immediately marking it down suggests they're doing exactly that math, and not liking all of it. It's not helped by a separate thread this week on Chinese AI firms getting weaned off Nvidia chips by export curbs, forcing Alibaba and Tencent to lean on homegrown chips instead. Tencent's president called the restrictions "unfortunate" and admitted it'll now take longer to train their next model. Put those together and you've got the two biggest AI growth stories, American and Chinese, both hitting friction in the same week.

Elsewhere, Intuit is selling Mailchimp for three point six eight billion dollars to Bending Spoons, the Berkshire Hathaway-backed acquirer, so it can pour resources into AI features across QuickBooks, Credit Karma and TurboTax. TPG and Corpay are buying the payments platform Bill Holdings for around six billion. And retail earnings are sending a split message: Walmart raised its outlook and says it's holding prices down despite rising fuel costs, while Gap cut its full-year guidance even as it named a new Athleta chief, and BJ's Wholesale saw one-off costs and promotions eat into an otherwise upbeat outlook.

That split is the pattern worth watching: strong headline guidance paired with cost pressure underneath, at Walmart, Gap and BJ's alike. Consumers are also trading up on clothing quality even as they pay more, according to new retail data. It suggests a consumer who's still spending but getting pickier and more price-sensitive at the same time. Watch whether holiday season numbers confirm that squeeze or whether it was one-off noise.
