Alex Ovechkin made more Capitals history last night, passing Nicklas Backstrom for the most assists in franchise history with his seven hundred and sixty-third career helper. It came in a losing effort, Washington fell six to three to Buffalo, but Ovechkin stopped to pay tribute to Backstrom afterward, the longtime teammate he just passed. At thirty-nine, in a season where people keep waiting for him to slow down, he keeps adding chapters to a résumé that's already the best scoring record in league history.

Elsewhere, the NFL's disciplinary news caught up with the Rams. Puka Nacua was hit with a three-game suspension for violating the personal conduct policy, stemming from an assault lawsuit, though he was already out with injury so the practical sting is dulled for now. It's still a notable stain on a receiver who's been one of the league's best stories the last two years. Indianapolis quarterback Daniel Jones had a night to forget in a three to twenty-six loss at Houston, his thirteen interceptions now lead the entire NFL, and some unwanted history to go with a two-year, eighty-eight million dollar contract. In the NBA, the Knicks won their third straight road game, beating Minnesota a hundred and two to ninety-nine behind a defense that locked in once their offense cooled off. And Victor Wembanyama is dealing with a painful blister that's forced him to adjust his dunking, though he's still on pace for an NBA record in slams this season.

The story with the most riding on it is Manchester United. Mauricio Pochettino says he's barely sleeping, racing to get an eighteenth-placed team ready for Sunday's trip to Liverpool, his first real test since taking over a club in genuine crisis.

Tonight, England discover Erling Haaland and Norway await in the Nations League quarterfinals, a rematch with real stakes down the road.
