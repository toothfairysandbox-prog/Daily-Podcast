Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia's chief executive, less than two weeks after the board accepted his resignation over an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. The Wall Street Journal reports Huang told the board there were more affairs than the two already public. He still got a hero's welcome back at headquarters. Trump had predicted he'd land on his feet; Elon Musk called it a corrected mistake. Politicians on both sides are calling the return outrageous and reckless. Megaspeed, the company at the center of the scandal, is reportedly headed for a US blacklist that would cut it off from American business. A widely shared investigation is also probing whether someone traded Polymarket contracts on the reinstatement before it went public. None of this changes Nvidia's business, but it's a real governance story for the industry's most important company.

Elsewhere, Amazon disclosed that hackers used an AI agent framework to find and exploit a zero-day vulnerability with almost no human input — offense catching up to defense. Anthropic launched Claude Ship, letting AI agents review and approve code changes themselves. And Uber, Nuro, and Lucid began fully driverless robotaxi testing on Bay Area public roads, dropping the human safety driver.

Treat the Polymarket insider-trading claim cautiously — it's one blogger's pattern analysis, not a regulatory finding.
