The Russia-Ukraine war is spreading geographically, and that's rattling NATO. Three Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday, and the Kremlin hasn't commented. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is trying to reassure eastern European members as Russian incursions into alliance territory keep increasing. Meanwhile Ukraine has taken the fight further afield: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian warship docked at a naval base in Libya, and he's acknowledged Ukraine is now targeting Russian vessels well outside the Black Sea. That's raising fears Libya's fragile reunification process could get dragged into a proxy fight. Separately, North Korea's promised troop buildup for Russia hasn't materialized, with analysts saying Pyongyang is hedging between Moscow and Beijing. Add in German media reports that Russia is planning to put a nuclear-armed satellite into orbit, which NATO has vowed to defend against, and the picture is of a conflict no longer contained to its original borders.

In Gaza, an Israeli air strike killed Naeem Abu Nahel, the director-general of the territory's Internal Security Service, just two months into the job. Six others died in strikes across the enclave, including two police officers and a seventeen-year-old. The ceasefire framework remains intact on paper, but Israel continues to target Hamas-linked officials inside it.

In Colombia, a car bomb wounded more than twenty people in the latest in a string of attacks on security and government buildings, as critics warn a security crackdown under Abelardo de la Espriella could unravel gains from the 2016 peace deal a decade on.

And in Central Africa, Congo and Kenya agreed to restore ties after years of strain over the conflict in eastern Congo, with presidents Tshisekedi and Ruto meeting to reset relations.

The slower-moving story is that Hormuz oil traffic is down forty percent as Iranian tanker attacks increase — a squeeze that hasn't broken into headlines but is reshaping shipping and insurance costs quietly.

Watch for whether the Kremlin ever responds to the Poland missile strikes, and whether NATO's reassurance to its eastern flank turns into anything more concrete.
