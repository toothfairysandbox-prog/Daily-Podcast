Jensen Huang is back running Nvidia, eleven days after his ouster over an alleged affair with a Chinese spy. The market loved it — nearly a trillion dollars added to Nvidia's value, the Nasdaq up six and a half percent — but the comeback came with baggage. Trump said he'd "convinced" the board to reinstate Huang, drawing backlash over presidential pressure on a corporate board. Analysts are split: some call it pragmatic given how irreplaceable Huang is, others say his judgment is still in question and the board owes the market a succession plan it never had. A reported SEC probe into finance chief Colette Kress adds more scrutiny.

Elsewhere, CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler for forty-four billion dollars in stock, consolidating cybersecurity around AI-driven threats. Sanofi struck the year's biggest pharma deal, forty-one billion for Insmed. And Best Buy posted its best comparable sales growth in five years, six point one percent, raising full-year guidance.

The pattern: concentration risk is testing governance everywhere, from one executive moving a trillion dollars to one company's bet reshaping a whole sector. A real Nvidia succession plan would ease that worry.
