Stocks are pulling back this morning after yesterday's big rally, and the two stories doing the damage are tariffs and Nvidia. Fresh tariff concerns are rattling investors again, just as markets had caught their breath. And Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO is proving messier than a simple comeback story — Congress is calling the board's decision a "failure of character," and there's still an internal investigation plus California and federal scrutiny hanging over the company. That's bleeding into the whole chip and AI trade. On the corporate side, Sanofi is buying drugmaker Insmed in pharma's biggest deal in three years, sending Insmed shares up fourteen percent, and Best Buy just posted its strongest comparable sales growth in five years, beating estimates and raising guidance.

Sixteen minutes into the session, the S&P 500 is down about half a percent, the Nasdaq is off over one percent as tech takes the brunt, but the Dow is actually up four tenths of a percent, and the Russell 2000 is down slightly too. Overseas, the real headline is Asia: Japan's Nikkei is up nearly four percent and South Korea's Kospi is up over six percent — both enormous moves, though we don't have the full story behind them yet. Europe's more subdued, with Germany's DAX down almost a percent.

Technology is the worst sector by far, down two and a half percent, dragged by the Nvidia uncertainty and a BNP Paribas downgrade of Micron over capacity risk through 2028. Health care is leading, up over two percent, likely getting a lift from the Sanofi-Insmed deal. Consumer staples and communication services are also holding up, which has the feel of money rotating into safer corners while chips wobble.

Treasuries are flat to slightly lower in yield: the two-year steady at four point six three percent, the ten-year unchanged at four point nine two percent, the thirty-year down two basis points to five point two nine percent. The curve's positive twenty-nine basis points, nothing dramatic there.

Worth watching today: how Nvidia trades as the political noise around Huang continues, and whether this tariff worry has legs or fades like past scares. If you read one thing, make it the piece on the six questions still hanging over Huang's return — the board reversal and Trump's role in it aren't going away.
