The number of newborns getting vaccinated for hepatitis B at birth is dropping sharply in the US. That shot matters because the virus can pass from mother to baby during delivery and, left untreated, hepatitis B can cause fatal liver disease years later. This isn't a new study with a sample size to weigh — it's surveillance data showing a real behavior shift, driven by the same vaccine hesitancy wave that's also hitting other childhood shots. The concerning part is timing: this is a birth-dose vaccine, meaning any gap shows up as actual pediatric liver disease down the line, not an abstract statistic.

Relatedly, doctors are describing an uphill fight against parents refusing vitamin K shots for newborns, which prevent a rare but severe bleeding disorder — another routine intervention now caught in the same current of disinformation. And heading into flu season, the CDC's vaccination tracking data is now coming out monthly instead of weekly, which doctors say could blind them to outbreaks in real time right when they need the picture sharpest.

On the industry side, Sanofi is buying the biotech Insmed for forty-one billion dollars, the largest pharma acquisition in years, picking up two approved drugs in the deal. And Roche's breast cancer pill just got its second FDA approval in a week, now cleared for early-stage disease after surgery — a legitimate expansion, not hype. Separately, the FDA took the rare step of citing two drugmakers for failing to report clinical trial results to a public database, the kind of accountability action that doesn't happen often.

On the overselling side: mushroom supplements are everywhere in wellness marketing promising gut, focus and immune benefits. The actual research backing those specific claims is thin and mostly preliminary — nowhere near what the marketing implies.

Worth reading: new work out of Stellenbosch University targeting the biological processes behind fungal virulence, a genuinely promising angle against infections that are getting harder to treat and have no good drug pipeline behind them.
