Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia's chief executive, less than two weeks after the board accepted his resignation over reports he'd had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. The Wall Street Journal now reports Huang admitted to the board there were more affairs than the two already public. He got a hero's welcome at Nvidia's headquarters anyway, telling employees he'll be "a better CEO the second time around." Trump had publicly predicted Huang would land on his feet, and Elon Musk cheered the reinstatement as a corrected mistake. Politicians from both parties are calling it outrageous and reckless. Meanwhile Megaspeed, the company at the center of the original scandal, is reportedly headed for a US blacklist that would cut it off from American business without special federal license. And a Hacker News investigation, sitting at 425 points, is digging into whether someone traded Polymarket contracts on Huang's reinstatement before it was public. None of this touches Nvidia's actual business, but it's a governance story about the most important company in the industry, and it isn't over.

Elsewhere, Amazon disclosed that hackers used an AI agent framework called Cairn to find and exploit a zero-day vulnerability with very little human guidance — one of the clearest signs yet that offense is catching up to defense on agentic tooling. Anthropic launched Claude Ship, letting AI agents review, fix, and approve code changes themselves, aiming to clear the bottleneck of human code review as AI-generated code piles up. And Uber, Nuro, and Lucid started fully driverless robotaxi testing on public roads in the Bay Area, dropping the human safety driver for the first time in this partnership.

The overhyped pick today: treat the Polymarket insider-trading investigation with some skepticism. It's gotten huge traction on Hacker News, but it's a single blogger's analysis of trading patterns, not a regulatory finding, and prediction markets move on rumor constantly.

Watch Megaspeed's blacklist status over the coming days — if it lands, it reshapes who Nvidia's customers can legally work with.
