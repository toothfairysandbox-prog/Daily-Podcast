Stocks are sliding this morning after yesterday's rally, with two stories driving it: fresh tariff concerns and fallout from Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO. Congress is calling the board's decision a "failure of character," and Nvidia still faces an internal investigation plus federal scrutiny — that's weighing on the whole chip trade. Elsewhere, Sanofi is buying Insmed in pharma's biggest deal in three years, and Best Buy posted its strongest sales growth in five years, beating estimates.

Sixteen minutes into the session, the S&P 500 is down about half a percent, the Nasdaq is off over one percent, but the Dow is up four tenths of a percent. Overseas, Japan's Nikkei is up nearly four percent and South Korea's Kospi is up over six percent — both massive moves. Europe's softer, Germany down almost a percent.

Technology is the worst sector, down two and a half percent, hurt by the Nvidia overhang and a Micron downgrade from BNP Paribas over 2028 capacity risk. Health care leads, up over two percent on the Sanofi deal, with staples and communication services also holding up as money rotates defensive.

Treasuries are little changed: the two-year at four point six three percent, the ten-year at four point nine two percent, the thirty-year down two basis points to five point two nine percent. The curve stays positive at twenty-nine basis points.
