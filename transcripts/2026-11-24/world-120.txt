The Russia-Ukraine war is spreading geographically. Three Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday, with no Kremlin comment, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is trying to reassure eastern European members as Russian incursions into alliance territory increase. Ukraine, meanwhile, says its sea drones sank a Russian warship docked in Libya, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has acknowledged targeting Russian vessels far from the Black Sea — raising worries about Libya's fragile political reunification getting pulled in. North Korea's promised troop buildup for Moscow still hasn't materialized, as Pyongyang balances ties with Russia and China.

In Gaza, an Israeli strike killed Naeem Abu Nahel, head of the territory's Internal Security Service, along with six others including two police officers and a teenager, in the latest strike inside the ceasefire framework.

In Colombia, a car bomb wounded more than twenty people, part of a string of attacks on security and government buildings, as a crackdown under President Abelardo de la Espriella raises fears for the 2016 peace deal.

Congo and Kenya agreed to restore ties after years of strain over the eastern Congo conflict.

The quieter story: Hormuz oil traffic is down forty percent amid rising Iranian tanker attacks, squeezing shipping costs with little daily attention.
