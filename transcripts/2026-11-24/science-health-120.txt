The number of newborns getting vaccinated for hepatitis B at birth is dropping sharply in the US — a real surveillance trend, not a study, and concerning because any gap here shows up later as actual liver disease, since the virus can pass from mother to baby at delivery. It's part of a broader pattern: doctors are also fighting parent refusals of newborn vitamin K shots, which prevent a rare but severe bleeding disorder. And heading into flu season, CDC vaccination tracking has shifted from weekly to monthly reporting, which doctors say could blind them to outbreaks right when timing matters most.

On the industry side, Sanofi is buying Insmed for forty-one billion dollars, the largest pharma acquisition in years. Roche's breast cancer pill just picked up a second FDA approval in a week, now for early-stage disease after surgery. And the FDA took the rare step of citing two drugmakers for failing to report clinical trial results publicly.

On the overselling side: mushroom supplements are marketed for gut health, focus and immunity, but the actual research backing those claims is thin and mostly preliminary.

Worth noting: Stellenbosch University researchers have identified a promising strategy targeting fungal virulence directly, relevant as drug-resistant fungal infections become a bigger clinical problem.
