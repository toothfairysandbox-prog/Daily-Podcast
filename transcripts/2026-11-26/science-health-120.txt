The Army detonated five tons of aging, unstable lead azide in Iowa and is calling it a success, with no lead detected in surrounding soil so far. A public health specialist told NPR that's an early read — monitoring needs to continue for weeks before the area gets a real clean bill. Worth tracking, not yet resolved.

Separately, the FDA approved Cogent Biosciences' new drug for a rare stomach cancer, paired with the older Pfizer drug Sutent. Narrow indication, but a genuine new option where few existed.

A psychology study found men and women test about equally capable at multitasking, but men were more than twice as likely to tune out a conversation while doing it — which may explain the long-running stereotype about women being better jugglers more than any actual skill gap does.

And a Thanksgiving health piece built around "best time to eat" research is stretching a real finding — that meal timing may matter alongside content — into advice for a single holiday dinner. The underlying science doesn't actually support that leap.

What to watch: ongoing soil and air testing at the Iowa site over coming weeks, since that's what will confirm or undercut Wednesday's "no lead detected" claim.
