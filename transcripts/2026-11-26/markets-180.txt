The story this morning is a global relief rally, with investors shrugging off both a fresh round of U.S. tariffs and the AI jitters that hammered tech earlier in the week. The White House unveiled new tariff threats on fourteen trading partners Wednesday, dangling lower rates for anyone who signs a deal with Washington. Markets took it in stride. Meanwhile, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE index, accelerated to three point eight percent in October — hotter than hoped, and it raises the stakes for whoever is setting policy into 2027. And Deutsche Bank is out with a call that the tech rout should bottom soon, with the S&P 500 reaching nine thousand by the end of 2027 as the AI story shifts from capital spending to actual monetization.

Fifteen minutes into the session, the S&P 500 is down a quarter percent, the Nasdaq is off six tenths, dragged by tech. But the Dow is up a third of a percent and the Russell 2000 is up seven tenths — small caps catching a bid. Overseas, it's a much bigger story: the DAX is up over two percent, the FTSE up one point six five, the CAC up one point seven, and the Nikkei, Hang Seng and most of Asia are all firmly green. This is a global bargain-hunting mood, not a U.S.-led move.

Materials lead U.S. sectors, up two percent, with real estate, communication services, industrials and energy all higher — energy tracking crude's gain. Technology is the laggard, down nearly a percent, with health care also soft. The split tells you this: cyclical, value-ish parts of the market are catching the rotation out of mega-cap tech, which is still digesting last week's AI wobble.

Treasuries are rallying. The ten-year yield is down six basis points to four eighty-five, the thirty-year down six to five twenty-four, the two-year down two to four sixty-one. The curve's steepening a touch — bonds reading the inflation print as noisy rather than a turning point, for now.
