Poland says it will create a no-fly zone along its border with Ukraine, after repeated drone incursions it blames on Russia. It's the clearest sign yet that Warsaw sees the airspace threat as serious enough to carve out a military buffer, not just lodge complaints. It comes as the war itself keeps spreading geographically: Ukraine has been striking Russian vessels as far afield as the Baltic and the Mediterranean, trying to squeeze Moscow's shipping and send a message to Western allies that it can reach beyond the battlefield. Russia, meanwhile, is developing its own satellite internet system, called Rasvet, explicitly to counter the edge Starlink has given Ukrainian forces. Both sides are widening the map of this war rather than narrowing it.

In eastern Congo, fighting between rebels and the army has forced Ebola responders to evacuate affected areas, just as the outbreak passes twelve thousand cases and more than five thousand seven hundred deaths — already the deadliest on record there. Health workers say the violence is actively disrupting containment efforts, which is about as bad a combination as an outbreak can have.

North Korea's Kim Jong Un has vowed to build more nuclear weapons, after effectively ignoring an overture from Donald Trump. He reportedly toured facilities that produce nuclear material and framed the buildup as necessary to counter threats from Washington and Seoul.

And Myanmar says it won't comply with parts of the International Court of Justice's genocide ruling against it over the Rohingya — a rare case of a state openly rejecting a binding world court judgment rather than just ignoring it quietly.

The slower story worth tracking is Hungary's realignment: new Prime Minister Peter Magyar met Volodymyr Zelensky this week, a sharp break from predecessor Viktor Orban's Kremlin-friendly years. If that holds, it reshapes the European Union's internal politics on Ukraine.

Watch Sunday's Spanish election, called after housing protests boiled over nationwide — it's shaping up as a referendum on Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez's handling of the crisis.
