Global stocks are rallying this morning, shrugging off fresh U.S. tariff threats on fourteen trading partners and the AI jitters that hit tech earlier in the week. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge came in hot, accelerating to three point eight percent in October, raising the stakes for 2027 policy. Deutsche Bank, meanwhile, says the tech rout should bottom soon, calling for the S&P 500 to hit nine thousand by the end of 2027 as AI monetization kicks in.

Fifteen minutes into the session, the S&P is down a quarter percent and the Nasdaq off six tenths on tech weakness, while the Dow is up a third and the Russell 2000 up seven tenths. Overseas is where the real action is: the DAX is up over two percent, the FTSE and CAC both up around one point seven, and Asian markets broadly green.

Materials lead sectors, up two percent, with real estate, industrials and energy also higher on crude's gain. Technology is the one soft spot, down nearly a percent — mega-cap AI names still digesting last week's selloff.

Treasuries are rallying too: the ten-year down six basis points to four eighty-five, the thirty-year down six to five twenty-four, the two-year down two to four sixty-one.
