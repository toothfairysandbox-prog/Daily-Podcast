Nvidia posted faster growth than expected in its first earnings call without Jensen Huang running the show. Interim chief Colette Kress delivered the numbers, and they were strong. But the company confirmed Huang won't be coming back, and the Wall Street Journal reports the SEC is now probing Nvidia around his departure. So you've got a business that's fundamentally firing on all cylinders, sitting underneath a governance story nobody's resolved. The Gulf states that bet heavily on Nvidia chips for their AI buildouts are watching closely too — any instability at the top complicates plans in Abu Dhabi and Saudi Arabia that assumed a steady hand.

Elsewhere in retail, the turnaround stories are real. Target posted its best sales growth in five years and raised guidance — actual evidence its fixes are working, not just a promise. Lowe's raised its outlook too, leaning on online sales even as the housing market stays sluggish. Both are signs consumers are still spending, just more selectively.

Oil slid to two-month lows on expectations of a supply surplus and easing Middle East tensions — good news if you're paying for gas or jet fuel, less so if you're an energy producer.

Across the board today, you're seeing the gap widen between companies that can show their turnaround in the numbers — Target, Lowe's, Nvidia — and everyone else still promising one. That's worth testing against: if even the weak links start posting beats next quarter, treat this as a true consumer recovery rather than just execution by the biggest players.

In the UK, inflation just hit its highest rate in a year, with typical household energy bills forecast to jump four hundred forty-three pounds a year starting in January — a twenty-six percent jump. That's going to squeeze discretionary spending right as retailers head into the holiday season.

Worth watching: more earnings and data this week as markets digest what a post-Huang Nvidia actually looks like, and whether UK inflation data changes the Bank of England's calculus heading into its next decision.
