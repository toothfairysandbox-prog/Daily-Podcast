San Antonio's twelve-game win streak is over. The Pistons went into the Alamo City and won it a hundred and five to ninety-three, and they did it despite Victor Wembanyama doing Victor Wembanyama things — twenty-five points, eighteen rebounds, and a self-assisted alley-oop off the backboard that's going to be on highlight reels all week. But Wembanyama sat for a stretch, and that's when Detroit, led by Cunningham and Jenkins, took over and buried San Antonio. The worse news for the Spurs: guard Dylan Harper, in the middle of a breakout first season as a starter, slipped on a wet spot late in the game and went down awkwardly with what looks like a groin injury. That's one to watch.

Elsewhere in the NBA, the Knicks beat the Raptors a hundred and eleven to a hundred and two, Toronto's fifteenth straight loss to New York. Minnesota got a steal from LaMelo Ball and a game-saving block from Trey Lyles to edge Portland, even with Ayo Dosunmu dropping a season-high thirty for the Wolves. And Luka Dončić's historic start to the season continued as the Lakers hosted Charlotte, though his availability against Utah tonight is reportedly going to come down to soreness.

On the cricket side, England finished off a statement series win over Australia, Ben Duckett's century and Sonny Baker's new-ball wickets powering a thumping ninety-seven run victory in the Hobart decider. But the bigger cricket story is off the field: Cameron Bancroft has directly accused Pat Cummins of knowing about the 2018 sandpaper plot before it happened, an explosive claim that's thrown Australian cricket into chaos and put real pressure on Cummins to respond.

The longer-arc story to watch is Milwaukee and Miami both looking worse for wear in the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade — the fit in Miami has been awkward, and the deal is looking more like an organizational mess for the Bucks the further it plays out.

Tonight, the Lakers host the Jazz, and Dončić's status is the thing to watch there.
