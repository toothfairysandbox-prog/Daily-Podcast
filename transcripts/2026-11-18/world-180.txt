Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are set to hold their first-ever three-way meeting on Thursday, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Xi greeted Trump on the tarmac when Air Force One landed, and all three leaders have now shaken hands publicly. The three come with very different agendas: Trump wants trade wins and leverage on Ukraine and Iran, Xi wants to position China as the stable alternative to American unilateralism, and Putin arrives trying to escape the fallout of a Ukrainian attack that partially collapsed a prized Russian bridge. Xi used his summit speech to warn against protectionism and unilateralism, widely read as a pointed jab at Washington. Nothing is expected to be signed, but the optics alone, three rival powers at one table, are the story.

Elsewhere, the Gulf is heating up again. Kuwait says it repelled an Iranian attack for the first time in two months, and Bahrain reports it was also hit, both following a US air strike on Iran's Qeshm island. It's a reminder that the US-Iran standoff is less a ceasefire than a pause that keeps threatening to break.

In Ukraine, a Russian strike killed Rayana, a mother who'd joined the army in 2024 to protect her son, one of many such losses as the war grinds on with no resolution in sight.

The slower story worth tracking: North Korea's Yongbyon enrichment facility has finished external construction on a major extension, the IAEA says, calling it cause for serious concern. There's no dramatic headline here, just steady, quiet expansion of the North's nuclear capacity that keeps getting pushed down the priority list.

Watch Thursday closely: the International Court of Justice rules on whether Myanmar's military committed genocide against the Rohingya, a case that could set precedent for how the same court eventually handles Israel's conduct in Gaza, and reports suggest Washington may sanction the judges if the ruling goes against Myanmar.
