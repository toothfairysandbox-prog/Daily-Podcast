Nvidia posted faster growth than expected in its first earnings call without Jensen Huang, delivered by interim chief Colette Kress. The results were strong, but Huang confirmed he won't return, and the Wall Street Journal reports the SEC is now probing the company around his exit. A business performing well, sitting under an unresolved governance question — and Gulf states that bet heavily on Nvidia chips are watching nervously too.

In retail, the evidence is in the numbers rather than the promises. Target posted its best sales growth in five years and raised guidance. Lowe's raised its outlook too, leaning on online sales despite a sluggish housing market. Both suggest consumers are still spending, just more selectively.

Oil slid to two-month lows on expectations of a supply surplus and easing Middle East tensions, good news for anyone paying at the pump.

The pattern worth naming: companies that can show a turnaround in the numbers are pulling ahead of those still just promising one. Watch whether weaker retailers start beating estimates too — that would signal a broader consumer recovery, not just strength at the top.

In the UK, inflation hit its highest rate in a year, with typical energy bills forecast to jump four hundred forty-three pounds annually from January — a twenty-six percent rise that will squeeze spending right into the holidays.
