Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin hold their first-ever three-way meeting Thursday at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Xi greeted Trump on the tarmac; all three have now shaken hands publicly. Trump wants trade leverage and movement on Ukraine and Iran; Xi wants to look like the stable alternative to American unilateralism, and used his summit speech to warn against protectionism, widely read as aimed at Washington; Putin arrives trying to move past a Ukrainian strike that partially collapsed a prized Russian bridge. No deals are expected, but three rival leaders at one table is the story.

The Gulf is heating up again: Kuwait says it repelled an Iranian attack for the first time in two months, and Bahrain says it was also hit, both after a US strike on Iran's Qeshm island. In Ukraine, a Russian strike killed Rayana, a mother who'd joined the army in 2024 to protect her son, one of many such losses as the war grinds on.

Worth tracking quietly: North Korea's Yongbyon enrichment facility has finished external construction on a major extension, the IAEA says, calling it cause for serious concern.

Watch Thursday: the International Court of Justice rules on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, a case that could set precedent for Gaza, amid fears Washington may sanction the judges if it rules against Myanmar.
