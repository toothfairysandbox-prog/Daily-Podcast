The United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, a designation it's held since 2000. The Pan American Health Organization made the call because a measles strain has circulated continuously in the country for more than a year, the formal threshold for "endemic" rather than "eliminated." The drivers are record case counts, falling vaccination rates in several states, and outbreaks that kept reigniting before being contained. It's a public-health bookkeeping shift more than an overnight danger, but it reflects a real erosion, and it'll likely fuel vaccine-policy debates going forward.

Two more items. A Finnish study following children for eight years found heavier screen use was associated with better cognitive processing in the teen years — contrary to the usual assumption, and a reminder that content likely matters more than total hours. And new brain-imaging research finds autistic and non-autistic brains look similar on average, but autistic individuals vary far more from each other, especially in social-processing regions — which may explain why past group-average studies missed real differences.

Worth being careful with: that autism finding is solid descriptive imaging work, not a diagnostic tool or treatment lead, despite how it may get framed.

Worth watching: flu cases are rising ahead of Thanksgiving travel season.
