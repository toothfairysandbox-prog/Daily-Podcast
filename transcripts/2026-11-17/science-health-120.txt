The FDA approved a new breast cancer drug from Roche, a meaningful new option for some patients and one the company hopes becomes a blockbuster. The real test comes once it's used widely, not just in trials.

Elsewhere, scientists linked Neanderthal DNA to lower schizophrenia risk in modern humans — a genetic association, not a mechanism, but another sign ancient interbreeding still shapes mental health today. Hormone therapy after menopause may improve cognition, researchers found, though the benefit depends heavily on which hormone and on genetics — not a blanket case for treatment. And a new review ties biological sex, genetics and menopause to differences in Alzheimer's risk and conditions like fibromyalgia, including surprises in immune and gut-brain activity. It's framework-building, pointing toward personalized treatment rather than delivering one yet.

On policy: the FDA created a new regulatory category for the supplement NAC, and NIAID's acting director, John Powers the Third, was made permanent.

Don't hear the hormone therapy finding as a simple green light — the benefit was genotype- and formulation-specific, far from a general recommendation.

Watch how Roche's new drug gets priced and adopted, and whether the Alzheimer's sex-difference work turns into actual treatment trials.
