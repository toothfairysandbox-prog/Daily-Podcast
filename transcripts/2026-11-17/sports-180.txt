The Los Angeles Chargers were winless through nine weeks. Monday night they beat the Baltimore Ravens, twenty-three to twenty, and it wasn't close to a fluke. Justin Herbert and Omarion Hampton both ran for first-half touchdowns, and the Chargers just manhandled Baltimore up front all night. For the Ravens, this is their fifth straight loss, and it's getting hard to call it a slump — it's a crisis. Jacoby Brissett made his Ravens debut at quarterback and the run defense was a sieve. The report cards are brutal, and the Steelers, who benefit directly in the standings, saw their playoff odds jump overnight.

Around the rest of the league, Monday's result ripples into a power-rankings shakeup, with Cleveland's late-season form drawing real attention as a playoff sleeper. In the NBA, Philadelphia is back on top of the Eastern Conference after sweeping a back-to-back — Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown both had big scoring nights in a win at Brooklyn, though Brown left bleeding after a head and hamstring collision with Julius Randle; LeBron James sat that one out. Cleveland's role players stepped up in a win over Chicago while injuries bite into the rotation. And Golden State got walloped by New Orleans, with Steve Kerr already signaling lineup changes are coming.

The real story in Baltimore isn't one loss — it's a five-game collapse with a shaky run defense and a quarterback change mid-season, and the playoff math is now genuinely ugly.
