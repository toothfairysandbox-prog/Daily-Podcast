Nvidia is at the center of everything this morning. CNBC reports the company is warning chip buyers about legal risk tied to China access, right as Commerce moves to tighten export controls further, and separately, U.S. agencies say they've been investigating Nvidia partner Megaspeed since 2024. Nvidia itself ruled out any Jensen Huang comeback campaign chatter, and in its own 13-F, it boosted its SpaceX stake while cutting Intel holdings by a quarter. All of this lands right before Nvidia earnings, which is why futures are soft and the whole AI trade is under pressure. Separately, Iran is escalating threats against U.S. forces as a third American carrier arrives in the Gulf and a tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz — that's a real geopolitical risk-off driver layered on top of the tech jitters.

That combination hit stocks hard in the last session. The S&P fell nearly two percent, the Nasdaq dropped two and a half percent, the Dow lost one point four percent, and the Russell 2000 was down one point seven percent. Overseas, Japan's Nikkei fell one point three percent after data showed its economy unexpectedly contracted last quarter on elevated energy costs — partly a legacy of the Iran war disrupting energy prices. South Korea's KOSPI dropped two point six percent, hit hardest by the same chip-sector unwind.

Technology was the worst sector by far, down three point two percent, as the Nvidia headlines and AI-bubble worries hit the whole complex — Stanley Druckenmiller, for what it's worth, has already been trimming AMD and Sandisk while rotating into SpaceX and Palantir. Energy and industrials also fell over one and a half percent each. On the other side, consumer staples and utilities were the only sectors in the green, a classic defensive rotation when risk appetite disappears.

Treasuries actually rallied on that risk-off mood: the two-year fell five basis points to four point eight three percent, the ten-year dropped three basis points to five point one two, and the curve stayed positively sloped at twenty-nine basis points — a sign bonds are pricing some growth caution, not a hiking fear.

The VIX jumped nearly nineteen percent to almost twenty-three, confirming the nervousness. Watch Nvidia's earnings and any Commerce Department follow-through on export rules — those are the two things that could move this market most today.
