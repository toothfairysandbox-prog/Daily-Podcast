Nvidia's board has told employees, bluntly, that it is not considering putting Jensen Huang back as chief executive. That's despite a letter from the board itself warning of "irreplaceable" talent loss, pressure from billionaire allies, and now Donald Trump publicly backing a "Bring Jensen Back" campaign. The company is dismissing the campaign even as its own directors seem to be arguing both sides. Underneath the drama: inquiries into Huang's relationship with a woman suspected of working with Chinese intelligence are still proceeding, and that's almost certainly why the board won't budge no matter who's asking. Interim chief Colette Kress now has to run Nvidia's first earnings call without Huang, and Wall Street is going to be looking past the chip numbers for signs of a leadership vacuum.

Elsewhere, Chase and Wells Fargo customers spent part of Monday locked out of their own money after a software bug Chase insists wasn't systemic or cyber-related. It's the second tech failure in days, coming right after Microsoft and the FAA both went dark and grounded flights nationwide. Airlines are now tallying the cost of that ground stop — more than twenty thousand flights canceled, with United also fighting its own separate outage affecting pilot communications. In deal news, Quebecor has made a twenty-two billion dollar bid for Telus, a move that would stitch together a true pan-Canadian telecom company. And California's attorney general is suing OpenAI, alleging it misled the public and endangered safety in connection with an attack on Hugging Face.

What ties a lot of this together is fragility in systems everyone assumed were solid — airline operations, bank backends, and now, corporate governance at one of the most valuable companies on earth. I'd watch for whether these outages turn out to share a root cause; if they're truly unrelated coincidences, that's one story, but a pattern of common vendor failure is a very different one.
